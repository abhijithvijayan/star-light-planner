    How the numbers are worked out

    Axes. Origin at the centre of the star, x to the right, y up, z towards the viewer. Front face at z = +depth/2, back face at z = −depth/2, all five tips at z = 0 where the faces are welded.

    Inner pentagon. Four 53 cm sides and one 58 cm bottom side don't fix a pentagon on their own (it can still flex). The model takes the version whose five corners lie on one circle, which is mirror-symmetric and the closest to a regular pentagon. Every edge length you gave is matched exactly.

    Points. Each tip sits on the perpendicular through the middle of its inner edge, at the distance that makes all four bars (front-left, front-right, back-left, back-right) exactly 80 cm, or 112 cm for the bottom point. Seen from the side, each point is the 80 / 80 / 27 (or 112 / 112 / 27) triangle in the spec.

    Points + centre. Each point is a four-sided pyramid with a base of inner-edge × depth. The wire goes round it in rings that get smaller towards the tip, one ring per pitch measured along the 80 or 112 cm bar. The centre is then wrapped as a pentagonal box, front face, side, back face, side, one ring per pitch of height.

    Full-body spiral. One wire spiralling up a vertical axis from the bottom tip, one ring per pitch of height. Each ring is the taut loop around the frame at that height, which follows the side notches because they open sideways. At the top corner of the inner pentagon the star splits into the two top points, so the wire spirals up one top point, runs back down to the notch, and spirals up the other. That keeps the V between the top points open. The return run is included in the total.

    Why not one loop round both top points. Above the notch, a single loop round both top points crosses up to 125 cm of empty air near the tips and fills the V in, so the top reads as a flat-topped block. It also needs about 11% more wire.

    Ring length. Every ring includes the small extra length from climbing one pitch (√(perimeter² + pitch²)). Wire slack, sag, the lead to the plug and any return run are not included; the wastage % is there for that.
